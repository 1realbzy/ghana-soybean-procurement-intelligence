# Problem definition

## Statement

**Proposed problem (validation stage):** Ghanaian soybean processors often struggle to reliably secure sufficient quantities of *suitable* locally produced soybeans for crushing, in a supply system that is geographically fragmented and informationally opaque.

## What is KNOWN (evidence-supported)

1. **Crush capacity exceeds recent utilisation.** USDA industry survey material reports ~13 large-scale processors with ~172,000 MT/year installed grain capacity and ~72,000 MT/year actual throughput (<50%). Processors are described as running below ~70% capacity due to insufficient grain (USDA GAIN GH2023-0006; GH2024-0006).
2. **Demand is feed-led.** More than 85% of soybean consumption is attributed to animal feed (poultry and aquaculture) (MoFA PFJ 2.0 via USDA GH2024-0006).
3. **Meal imports remain material** even when bean self-sufficiency KPIs look strong. USDA Post SBM imports ~57–60k MT in recent marketing years; WITS HS 2304 import value US$44.42m in CY 2023.
4. **Trade policy can favour imported meal over domestic crush inputs.** Soybean grain faces 10% CIF duty; SBM duty is often waived in practice (USDA Oilseeds notes).
5. **Supply is northern and smallholder-heavy.** USDA describes production concentrated in northern Ghana with many farms under 1 ha.
6. **Formal market infrastructure moves little soy.** GCX traded 512 MT of soybeans in 2024 — negligible versus national production (Citinewsroom / FSR 2024 summary).
7. **Quality segmentation matters.** Poultry buyers often prefer imported solvent-extracted higher-protein meal over many local expeller meals, even when local meal is cheaper on a bag basis (USDA GH2024-0006 price points, Apr 2024).

## What is HYPOTHESIS

H1–H5 are listed in [`validation/hypotheses.md`](../validation/hypotheses.md). In short: limited digital visibility into supply location, timing, quantity, quality, price, and logistics is a *material and addressable* constraint on processor procurement—not merely a symptom of absolute production shortage.

## What is NOT claimed

- That every underutilised tonne of crush capacity is caused by information failure.  
- That processors have already confirmed this product concept.  
- That AI will raise utilisation or farmer incomes by a stated percentage.  
- That competing constraints (working capital, tariffs, export incentives, power, quality technology) are unimportant.

## Competing explanations (must be tested)

| Alternative constraint | Why it could dominate | How to check |
|------------------------|----------------------|--------------|
| Working capital to buy beans at harvest | Crushers cited capital needs (USDA / sector notes) | Interview CFOs; inspect purchase calendars |
| Export of beans competing with local crush | USDA export forecasts 50–60k MT beans; permits since 2021 | Trace offtake vs export shares |
| Quality / solvent vs expeller gap | Poultry preference for imports | Sample meal specs vs buyer requirements |
| Tariff asymmetry | Documented duty practice | Policy review with processors |
| Logistics north→crush belt | Structural geography | Cost and lead-time mapping |
| Information / coordination failure | **This project’s wedge** | Interviews + time-motion of current procurement |

## Success condition for the problem framing

The framing is useful only if validation shows processors would change procurement behaviour when given trustworthy, timely supply intelligence—and that enough consented data can be assembled to make that intelligence non-hallucinated.

# Procurement bottlenecks

Mapped along: **requirement → find supply → verify quality → agree price → finance → move → receive → crush**.

| Bottleneck | Evidence status | Notes for validation |
|------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Knowing *where* beans of suitable quality will be available | **HYPOTHESIS** | Core product wedge; interview procurement officers |
| Timing vs harvest calendar and moisture/quality windows | **HYPOTHESIS** + seasonal production KNOWN | Need harvest calendars by district |
| Quantity commitments from fragmented smallholders | KNOWN structure; commitment failure rate **UNKNOWN** | Need default/reneging data |
| Quality (protein, oil, foreign matter; solvent vs expeller suitability) | KNOWN buyer preference for imported solvent meal | Lab/spec gap analysis |
| Price discovery outside thin GCX volumes | KNOWN (GCX 512 MT soy 2024) | Map informal price channels |
| Competition from export offtake | KNOWN export volumes/values | Share of northern crop exported vs crushed |
| Working capital at harvest | SECONDARY / USDA narrative | Separately from information tools |
| North→crush logistics and lead times | ANALYST INTERPRETATION | Cost/time study |
| Tariff / policy incentives to import meal | KNOWN | Policy may dominate software |
| Trust and aggregation (side-selling) | Common in outgrower literature; **Ghana soy-specific rate INSUFFICIENT** | Interviews |

## Implications for product design

1. Intelligence that ignores **quality specs** and **export competition** will be incomplete.  
2. A tool that only scrapes public MoFA PDFs will not beat a buyer’s phone network unless it adds timely, consented microdata.  
3. If validation shows working capital or tariffs dominate, the product should narrow to decision-support around *when/where to deploy scarce cash*, not claim to “solve utilisation.”
